## Introduction

This research examines the effectiveness of heuristic-based risk scoring for smart contracts, using Contrax as the primary test implementation across EVM and Solana chains.

## Background

Most existing contract-safety tools rely on either static analysis of verified source code or crowd-sourced blocklists, both of which fail for freshly deployed, unverified contracts.

## Objectives

- Identify signals available before source verification exists.
- Evaluate false-positive and false-negative rates for common heuristics.
- Determine which signals generalize across EVM and Solana.

## Methodology

A set of 12 heuristics — ownership renouncement, mint authority, liquidity lock status, and holder concentration among them — were applied to a sample of 250 contracts with known outcomes, split evenly between EVM and Solana.

## Results

Ownership and mint-authority signals produced the lowest false-positive rates on EVM. Solana's account model required different equivalents, since there is no direct analog to contract ownership.

## Discussion

No single heuristic was reliable enough to stand alone. Combining signals into a reported set, rather than a collapsed score, produced results users trusted more in informal testing.

## Conclusion

Heuristic scoring is a useful first line of defense, but should be presented as a set of individual, inspectable signals rather than a single confidence number.
