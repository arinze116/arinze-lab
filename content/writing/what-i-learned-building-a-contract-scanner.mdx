What I Learned Building a Contract Scanner

Heuristic risk scoring for smart contracts is harder than it looks. Some lessons from building Contrax.

**The naive first version**

The first version of Contrax checked for a handful of obvious red flags, unverified source, no liquidity lock, that kind of thing, and boiled it all down to a single score from 0 to 100. It felt useful, right up until I started running it against contracts I already knew were safe and watched it get confused anyway.

**Where scoring breaks down**

A single number hides more than it reveals. Two contracts can land on the exact same score while carrying completely different kinds of risk, one flagged for mint authority issues, the other for proxy risk, and collapsing both into one figure just misleads people rather than helping them. I didn't realize how much nuance I was throwing away until I saw it happen in practice.

**The fix**

Contrax now reports individual signals instead of one composite score. It checks mint and freeze authority, proxy risk, runs honeypot simulation, and pulls market data, then shows each result on its own instead of averaging them into something meaningless. And when it genuinely can't evaluate something with confidence, it says so, marking it unknown instead of guessing and hoping. That took real effort, since the analyzer covers both EVM contracts through Etherscan and Solana through Helius, and each chain breaks in its own particular way. Fixing the classification bugs and rate-limit failures along the way only reinforced the same point: false confidence is worse than an honest gap.

**The bigger lesson**

A tool that says "I don't know" when it doesn't know is more trustworthy than one that always has an answer. That's a strange thing to optimize for when everyone wants a clean number, but it's the difference between a scanner people can actually rely on and one that just looks convincing until it's wrong.
